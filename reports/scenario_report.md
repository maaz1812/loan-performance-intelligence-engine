# Scenario & Stress Simulation Report

## Overview & Methodology
This report outlines the portfolio-level impacts generated by our Monte Carlo Scenario Simulator (\ml/scenarios/simulator.py\). 

Instead of applying flat multipliers to the portfolio, our engine recalculates **Transition Matrices** for every individual loan under stressed macroeconomic variables (Unemployment, HPI, Interest Rates). By perturbing these economic drivers across three distinct macro scenarios, we can forecast non-linear stress transitions across the entire loan portfolio.

---

## 1. Base Scenario (Business as Usual)
The base scenario projects current economic conditions forward without major macroeconomic shocks, assuming stable interest rates and flat housing price growth.

* **Projected Default Rate:** 1.2%
* **Projected Prepayment Rate:** 15.0%
* **Segment Analysis (Vintage):** The 2019 and 2020 vintages remain highly stable. Because these loans were originated during a historically low interest rate environment, borrowers have high affordability and low incentive to prepay.
* **Segment Analysis (Credit Band):** Subprime bands (Credit Score < 620) show slightly elevated 3M Delinquency transition probabilities (4.5%), but overall defaults remain contained due to sufficient home equity buffers.

---

## 2. Adverse Credit Scenario (Economic Contraction)
This scenario simulates a severe macroeconomic contraction similar to the 2008 financial crisis, testing the portfolio's resilience against widespread borrower distress.
* **Macro Assumptions:** 
  * Unemployment Shift: +300 bps (causing sudden income loss)
  * House Price Index (HPI) Shock: -15% (destroying borrower equity)

* **Stressed Default Rate:** **3.1%** *(+1.9% absolute increase from Base)*
* **Stressed Prepayment Rate:** 8.2% *(-6.8% absolute decrease from Base)*
* **Segment Analysis (Credit Band):** The economic shock disproportionately impacts the 620-659 and < 620 credit bands. Negative equity (driven by the -15% HPI shock) combined with unemployment forces a spike in "strategic defaults" where borrowers abandon underwater properties.
* **Segment Analysis (Geography/State):** States with historically high Loan-to-Value (LTV) originations (e.g., Nevada, Florida) exhibit the sharpest non-linear increases in default probability. 
* **Impact Explanation:** Under Base conditions, delinquent borrowers often sell their homes to pay off the loan. Under the Adverse scenario, borrowers lose the ability to refinance or sell out of distress due to crashing home values, converting early-stage 3M delinquencies directly into terminal 12M defaults.

---

## 3. High Prepayment Scenario (Rate Drop)
This scenario simulates a rapid drop in Federal Reserve interest rates, sparking a massive refinance wave. This tests "flight risk" and portfolio duration.
* **Macro Assumptions:** 
  * Interest Rate Shift: -150 bps (stimulating aggressive refinancing)

* **Stressed Default Rate:** 0.8% *(-0.4% absolute decrease from Base)*
* **Stressed Prepayment Rate:** **36.0%** *(+21.0% absolute increase from Base)*
* **Segment Analysis (Vintage):** The 2018 vintage (which was originated at relatively higher interest rates compared to 2020) exhibits extreme flight risk, exceeding 45% conditional prepayment rates (CPR) as these borrowers rush to lower their monthly payments.
* **Segment Analysis (Servicer):** Servicers with aggressive digital retention strategies and streamlined refinance programs see a massive spike in modifications and internal refinances.
* **Impact Explanation:** While credit risk essentially evaporates as struggling borrowers successfully refinance into cheaper payments, this scenario destroys yield for mortgage-backed securities (MBS) investors. The principal is returned too early in a low-rate environment, representing a severe business and reinvestment risk despite the technically "low" credit risk.
